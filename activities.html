On the water

Whales, waves and everything in between

Mirissa's biggest draw is what happens offshore — but there's a full slate of activities for every kind of traveller.

Signature activity

Blue whale & dolphin watching

The continental shelf drops steeply just off Mirissa, creating a deep-water trench close to shore. That geography draws blue whales, sperm whales and large pods of spinner dolphins within a short boat ride of the harbour, making Mirissa one of the most consistent places in the world to see a blue whale in the wild.

Boats leave the fisheries harbour early each morning during the season, when the water is calmest and sightings are most likely.

  • Tours typically run 3–4 hours and depart between 6:00 and 7:00am
  • Bring sun protection, a light jacket and motion-sickness tablets if needed
  • Choose an operator that keeps a respectful distance from the animals

Also on the water

Surfing at Weligama Bay

Weligama, just four kilometres west of Mirissa, is one of Sri Lanka's best-known beginner surf spots, with long, gentle rolling waves and plenty of board rental and lesson operators along the beach. More experienced surfers head slightly further along the coast for faster breaks.

Snorkelling gear can also be hired locally to explore the calmer rocky patches near Secret Beach and around Parrot Rock at low tide.

Galle Fort & ramparts

A short drive west along the coast to the UNESCO-listed Dutch fort, its ramparts, lighthouse and boutique streets.

1–2 hours

Cycling the coast road

Bicycles are easy to rent in town for an easy ride between Mirissa, Weligama and the small coves along the way.

Sunset trip

Dondra Head Lighthouse

Sri Lanka's southernmost point, a short drive east, with a working lighthouse and open sea views.

Anytime

Snorkelling near Parrot Rock

Calm, shallow rock pools at low tide make for easy, gear-light snorkelling close to the beach.

Booking tips

Book a day ahead

Popular whale-watching and safari operators fill up, especially December to February.

Go early

Morning departures mean calmer seas and better wildlife sightings.

Ask about group size

Smaller boats and groups usually mean a better, less crowded experience.
